# Remaining 200ms tail and first-preview / physical pacing work

T-196's single idle-deferred drain helps a deep 100-event queue in some
software-X11 observations, without extending the mouse-up callback. The final
four-observation cold p95 regresses (215.2→227.4ms), warm p95 remains 205.4ms,
and first-preview p95 also increases in the paired samples. The 200ms stretch
and full-workspace paced frame acceptance are **not achieved**.

## 2026-11-18: renderer-callback integrity checkpoint (in progress)

The first-preview metric previously accepted the next global
`RenderingServer.frame_post_draw` callback once `first_preview_submit_us`
was nonzero, even without a Canvas frame/active edit-session match. T-197
now stamps `first_frame_post_draw_us` **only** in the existing matched
frame-phase callback, after a Canvas frame submitted after first preview.
No Brush input, native staging, source or release scheduling was changed.

A fail-first Godot regression observed **3 failures/6 checks** in the
unstaged pre-fix Canvas; after restaging it passes **9/9**, including
real Hokusai first preview and one atomic native history commit on X11.
Two confirmed full Drawing workspace processes (16 scenarios total) capture
54%/100% cold/warm/held Brush and Eraser; source RGBA digests match in all
corresponding scenarios. The repeatable remaining hotspot is **Eraser
composition, 79–92ms cumulative**, versus **3–5ms for Brush**;
its transparent/hidden-RGB single-layer preview falls back to per-pixel
GDScript compositing. The benchmark evidence is observational, not a
same-binary performance A/B. No performance win or 200ms p95 has been
claimed; actual optimization and physical acceptance are still open.
